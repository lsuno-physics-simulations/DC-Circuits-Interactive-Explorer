    DC circuit

    Model, conventions, and textbook alignment

    This is a browser conversion of the MATLAB DC Circuit Explorer, aligned with Tipler and Mosca’s sixth-edition Chapter 25 (DC circuits) and the current-force introduction to Chapter 26 (magnetic field). It is an original teaching tool; specific textbook figures and problems are not reproduced. Fields generated by currents belong to a later Chapter 27 extension.

    DC networks use modified nodal analysis with ideal ohmic resistors. Each source may have a series internal resistance; both sources share the r setting. Node N1 is ground. Battery long plates identify the positive reference terminal; negative emf reverses the source relative to it. Signed branch currents follow the listed node-to-node reference; blue arrows show actual conventional current. Orange electron markers move oppositely at exaggerated speeds and do not model signal propagation.

    The potential plot is a component-by-component traversal of a closed loop, rather than a spatial electric-field profile. Positive power is absorbed; negative power is supplied. Ideal-source power and internal heating are listed separately, allowing a source to absorb power if another source drives it.

    RC transients use the exact solution Vc = E − (E − V₀)e−t/RC, I = (E − V₀)e−t/RC/R. Only R1, C, and E1 are used; the battery is ideal. Switching preserves Vc and restarts time and energy bookkeeping. Reset in discharge mode starts with Vc = E1. Editing parameters resets the experiment. Charge is Q = CVc; stored energy is ½CVc². Battery work equals capacitor-energy change plus resistor heat.

    In the magnetic activity, a fixed negligible-resistance wire carries I = E1/(R1+r) in an imposed uniform field Bz. Its reference vector is L = L(sin θ, 0, cos θ), and F = I(L × B). The angle is measured from +z. The field and force arrows show directions at normalized display lengths. There is no wire motion, induction, or back emf.

    All calculations run in your browser. There are no accounts, tracking, external libraries, persistent storage, or grade reporting. A downloaded HTML file works offline. The page starts paused and honors reduced-motion preferences for electron animation. Reload restores defaults.
